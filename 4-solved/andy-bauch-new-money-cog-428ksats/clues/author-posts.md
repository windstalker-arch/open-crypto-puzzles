# Author and gallery material, dated

## The exhibition press release, "New Money"

Archived Artsy show listing, 2018-09-21: [artsy.net/show/hijinx-new-money](http://web.archive.org/web/20180921114353/https://www.artsy.net/show/hijinx-new-money).

> "New Money will feature over a dozen works comprised of LEGO bricks, depicting
> everything from digitized abstractions to portraits of low wage workers who are
> in the process of being phased out by automation. Each artwork is imbued with
> secret keys to bitcoin wallets, with initial values ranging between $20 and $90.
> [...] that's just assuming a clever viewer doesn't reverse-engineer the Bitcoin
> pattern embedded in each piece and use it to empty out the digital wallet of its
> value."
>
> "New Money will be on view from March 23 - March 25, 2018, with an opening
> reception on March 23 from 7pm - 10pm. Castelli Art Space is located at 5428 W.
> Washington Blvd. Los Angeles, CA 90016."

The same listing's own catalog entry for COG shows a sale price of $14,000
(archived capture 2018-03-24) moving to "on hold" (2018-03-25, the show's last
day) and "Sold" by the 2018-09-21 capture used above.

## On the two ways the artist builds a piece (UPROXX studio interview, 2017-11-21)

> "I'll take either a pre-existing image, or something that I've generated using
> an algorithm, or something that I've designed. I'll embed data in it a lot of
> the time, and then I actually create the work after that."
>
> "In the case of these pieces, this software actually fully generates a pattern
> from the secret keys to the bitcoins."
>
> "The pieces I've made take a Bitcoin secret key and they expose it for all the
> world to see. The money is actually hidden in the pattern created by the Lego."

The abstract "Bitcoin Initially Valued at $N" pieces use the second approach
(an algorithm generates the whole pattern from the key). COG is a photographic
portrait, not a generated abstraction, which by the artist's own framing points to
the first approach: an existing image with data embedded in it.

## The artist confirms COG is still unsolved (ESCAPUZZLED Podcast episode 7, 2024-08-14)

> "I think there's one piece from the show New Money that I did which is unsolved,
> and it was like a triptych, sort of like a sales retail clerk who is sort of like
> almost evaporating over the course of this triptych. [...] that one I think
> remains unsolved."
>
> "I put the crypto in there a lot later than like the 2016 ones, so don't get
> too excited, but I think the puzzle is still waiting."
>
> "I think that one's called Cog on my website, and it's much, much harder in
> some ways than the other pieces on there."
